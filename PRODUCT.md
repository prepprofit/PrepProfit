# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Solo chefs/owners and small restaurant teams (2–5 people). The solo owner uses it at night after service — laptop on the kitchen counter, tired, needs answers fast. The team manager uses it in the office during prep hours, focused on margins and invoicing. Both are experts in food, not in finance; the product exists so they don't need to be.

## Product Purpose

PrepProfit replaces the spreadsheet for food-business financial management: recipe costing, inventory, break-even, invoicing, payroll. It makes the numbers that keep a restaurant alive — food cost %, margin per dish, daily break-even — visible and actionable without an accountant. Success is a chef who knows their numbers without needing Excel.

## Brand Personality

Precise. Grounded. Unapologetically professional.

Not a recipe app. Not a POS. A serious financial tool that happens to understand kitchens. The tone is direct and confident — like a CFO who trained as a chef. No cheerfulness for its own sake. No lifestyle photography. Numbers are the product.

## Anti-references

- **Typical AI SaaS 2024**: cream/sand bg, radial hero gradient, identical card grid, eyebrow badges on every section, numbered step circles. This is the current design; it must be eliminated entirely.
- **Toast/Lightspeed/Square**: POS-hardware aesthetic, blue corporate palette, tablet screenshots. PrepProfit is finance software, not a POS.
- **QuickBooks/Sage**: bureaucratic gray, dense tables, visually intimidating. PrepProfit is approachable but not childish.

## Design Principles

1. **Numbers are the hero.** The product is about financial clarity. Surface real data — a margin figure, a cost percentage, a break-even threshold — before you explain features.
2. **Earn trust before you sell.** A food-business owner has been burned by software that promised simplicity and delivered complexity. Confidence comes from showing, not telling.
3. **Premium means restraint.** One color does the work. Space is not wasted. Typography carries the hierarchy, not decorative elements.
4. **Kitchen-native, not kitchen-themed.** Understand the context (late nights, tight margins, no time to read) without reaching for food photography or chef illustrations.
5. **The spreadsheet is the enemy.** Every section should make a spreadsheet feel like a step backward.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Body text ≥ 4.5:1 contrast. Reduced-motion respected on all animations. Keyboard navigable. No color-only information encoding (important for reading profit/loss without relying on red/green alone).
